Water heater installation in Simsbury, CT is the work of setting a new unit where none stood before, moving an existing one, or changing its fuel or type. It includes the supply and drain connections, the gas or electric feed, the venting, the relief valve piping and a leak and temperature test. Done on a calm weekday, it gives you time to choose well.

That is the main difference from a failed-tank call. If yours has already given out, emergency tank replacement is the faster route, and the 24/7 line is there for it. This page is for the install you control.

Below you will find how conversions work, what a heat pump unit needs, how to compare quotes line by line, and how to check a plumber's licence with the state.

Moving or adding a heater

New installs come up in finished basements, garage conversions, additions and houses where the old heater sits in a spot that is hard to service. A relocation means new supply and return lines, a drain, a vent route or electrical run, and a pan and drain path that make sense for the new space.

Think about the distance to the fixtures. A heater far from the kitchen and bathrooms means a long wait for hot water, which wastes water too. Putting the unit central, or adding a recirculation loop, can fix that, and the plumber can show you both options on site.

Gas to electric, tank to tankless: conversions compared

A conversion is the one place where an install turns into a small construction project. Pick the direction before the quote, since each has different hidden work.

Gas tank to electric or heat pump

  • Needs a dedicated circuit, which may mean an electrician for the panel side.
  • The gas line to the heater is capped by a licensed plumber and the old flue is removed.
  • A hybrid heat pump unit also needs a condensate drain and room to breathe.
  • Running costs shift from the gas bill to the electric one.

Tank to tankless

  • Gas models often need a larger gas line and new venting, and some need a condensate drain.
  • DOE notes that homes with simultaneous hot water use may need two or more units, and a recirculation loop can shorten the wait.
  • The wall space replaces the tank footprint, so floor space comes back.
  • Read about tankless water heater service before you commit, because annual descaling is part of ownership.

Heat pump water heaters and the space they need

DOE describes heat pump water heaters as moving heat from surrounding air into the tank rather than generating heat directly, and says most are hybrids with a backup electric element. Its guide says an ENERGY STAR certified model uses 70% less electricity than a standard electric tank. DOE also says one in a basement can dehumidify in summer but can pose installation challenges, so talk to an installer first.

In practice that means checking the room before choosing the model. The unit needs clearance for airflow, a place for condensate to drain, and a room that does not drop too cold in winter. A cramped furnace closet is usually a poor fit, while an open basement corner often works. Ask the installer to measure and photograph the space, and to explain what happens if the room gets chilly.

Venting, combustion air, pans and relief piping

Venting

A gas tank exhausts through a flue, a power-vent unit through plastic pipe, and a direct-vent unit through a sealed pipe pair. Each has size and length limits set by the maker. EPA lists blocked, disconnected or leaking flues among carbon monoxide sources.

Combustion air

An atmospheric gas heater draws air from the room. A tight utility closet can starve the burner and cause back-drafting. Ask how the installer is providing air.

Drain pan

A pan with a drain line catches slow leaks from a tank above finished space. It does not replace checking the unit once in a while.

T and P relief discharge

The temperature and pressure relief valve needs a discharge pipe. EPA WaterSense says to check the valve yearly. We do not quote a state rule on routing it here, so ask the installer to show you the run and ask the Simsbury building department whether any local requirement applies.

Comparing install quotes line by line

Two quotes can look the same and cover very different work. Lay them side by side and check these points.

  • Capacity, fuel and model type are named, not just a brand.
  • Venting materials and any flue replacement are listed.
  • An expansion tank, drain pan, new shutoffs and a new relief valve are included or marked as extras.
  • Removal and disposal of the old tank is spelled out.
  • The quote says who handles any permit or inspection the Simsbury building department requires.
  • Warranty terms for the unit and for labor are in writing, and the plumber's state credential is on the paperwork.

Check the plumber's licence with the state

Connecticut's Department of Consumer Protection runs an eLicense lookup at elicense.ct.gov, and it calls the data a primary source of verification. Search by name, business or credential and look for an active status. A P-1 is the unlimited plumbing contractor licence, and state statute says licensed work cannot even be advertised without the licence, which must be shown on request.

Plumbing licences expire each year on October 31, so a card that shows last year's date should be checked online. DCP also takes complaint inquiries at 1-800-842-2649.

State statute does exempt owners doing plumbing work in a single-family home they live in, but the work is subject to inspection and approval by the municipal building official and must meet the State Building Code. With gas connections and venting, many owners still hire it out. Ask the Simsbury building department what applies to your project.

Getting ready for install day

A little preparation keeps the visit short.

1

Clear the path

Move boxes from around the old unit and from the route to the stairs, so the heater can travel both ways.

2

Photograph the setup

Take pictures of the nameplate, the vent, the gas valve, the panel and the shutoffs for the quote.

3

Know your main valve

Find and test the main water shutoff before the plumber arrives. If it sticks, say so.

4

Plan for no water

Fill a few containers for drinking and flushing, and tell the household when the supply goes off.

How an install visit runs

1

Site walkthrough

The plumber checks location, venting path, supply lines and electrical or gas capacity, then gives a clear price.

2

Prepare and remove

Water and fuel are shut off, the old unit drained and disconnected, and the area cleaned.

3

Set and connect

The new unit goes on its pan, piping, valves, expansion tank and vent are connected.

4

Test and walk through

Leak checks, combustion or element tests and temperature checks come before a walkthrough of shutoffs and settings.

Limits, gas safety and neighbors

Some pieces sit outside a plumber's scope. A new circuit or a panel change belongs to an electrician, and a chimney liner problem may need a mason or chimney specialist. If you ever smell gas near the heater, leave the building and call Connecticut Natural Gas at 866-924-5325 or 911 before anyone calls a plumber.
