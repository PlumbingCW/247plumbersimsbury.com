A failed water heater in Simsbury, CT rarely gives warning. You find a spreading puddle in the basement or a closet full of rusty water, and the shower has gone cold. The 24/7 line takes these calls at any hour. While you wait, close the cold supply valve above the tank, shut off its breaker or gas control, and stay away from any outlet or panel that is standing in water.

Water heater replacement is the full swap of a failed unit. The old tank is drained and removed, a new one is set and leveled, and the water lines, fuel or power, venting, relief valve and drain are reconnected and tested. Done properly it is a job for a licensed plumber, because gas and water piping work is regulated in Connecticut.

Same-day replacement is offered when stock and the schedule allow. After-hours and weekend calls reach the same dispatcher.

Failed tank flooding the floor: what to do first

Contain it, then collect details

A split tank can release dozens of gallons quickly, so stopping the supply matters more than cleanup.

  • Turn the cold inlet valve clockwise until it stops. If it will not budge, shut the main water valve instead.
  • Kill the power at the breaker, or set a gas control to off. Gas smell means leave the house and call Connecticut Natural Gas at 866-924-5325 or 911.
  • Photograph the data plate on the side of the tank, which shows the capacity and fuel, and note how the flue or vent leaves the room. This lets the plumber arrive with a matching unit.
  • Lift cardboard, rugs and stored boxes off wet concrete and run a mop or wet vacuum.

Choosing size, fuel and venting

The fastest swap is a like-for-like one, so the plumber will usually start by matching your current unit. If the household has grown or shrunk, say so. Capacity should follow how many people shower and run laundry at once, and a bigger tank is not automatically better, since it costs more to keep warm.

Fuel type

Simsbury is listed under Connecticut Natural Gas on the state utility regulator's coverage table. Gas units draw from that service, while electric units need a suitable dedicated circuit. Changing fuel is possible, but it adds gas piping, venting or electrical work, so it is better planned than done mid-emergency. A planned installation is the better route for conversions.

Venting and combustion air

A gas tank has to exhaust through a flue sized and pitched for that appliance. EPA lists back-drafting from gas water heaters and blocked or disconnected flues as carbon monoxide sources, so the old vent is inspected rather than reused on trust. Rusted flue pipe, loose joints or a crushed liner get replaced with the heater.

Expansion tank and valves

Many homes now have a check valve or pressure reducing valve on the supply, which closes the system. Heated water has nowhere to expand, and pressure climbs. An expansion tank absorbs it, and a new heater is the right moment to add or replace one. The T and P relief valve and shutoffs are replaced too, so they match the new unit.

Connecticut code, permits and your gas utility

Connecticut's 2022 State Building Code is in effect, and its plumbing edition is the 2021 International Plumbing Code with Connecticut amendments dated October 1, 2022. The state notes a newer code is expected but has not confirmed a date, so the 2022 edition governs today. Ask the Simsbury building department whether a permit applies to your swap, since we do not want to guess at local rules for you.

Gas units tie into Connecticut Natural Gas service. The utility's emergency line is 866-924-5325, and its non-emergency number is on your bill. Never relight a unit after a gas shutoff, and leave gas piping to a licensed plumber.

Insurers separate sudden water damage from wear and tear. A sudden discharge may be covered, but the Insurance Information Institute says policies generally do not pay for damage from lack of maintenance, and the old heater is usually yours to replace. Check with your insurer, and keep photos of the damage.

What this job does not include

Replacing a heater does not dry a flooded basement or treat mold, and those are restoration jobs. If your water has been seeping for weeks, a leak survey can confirm whether the tank is the only source. A split pipe beside the heater needs burst pipe repair rather than a new tank. An electric unit that needs a bigger circuit also needs an electrician.
