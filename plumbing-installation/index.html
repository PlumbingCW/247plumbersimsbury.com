Plumbing installation in Simsbury, CT means connecting new water supply, drain and vent lines to fixtures and appliances, and moving existing lines when a room changes. It covers toilets, sinks, tubs and showers, laundry boxes, dishwasher and ice maker lines, outdoor spigots and water treatment equipment. The work follows the plumbing code that Connecticut has adopted, which is currently the 2021 International Plumbing Code with state amendments.

Most installs are planned, so you can schedule them in the day. If a failed fixture or a sudden leak forces the issue, our 24/7 emergency line is open, and same-day appointments are available when the schedule allows.

This page is about what to expect when you add or replace plumbing, what you need to supply and when state law says a licensed plumber must do the work.

What we install

Toilets and bidet seats

Water filters and softeners

Mounting, bypass valves and drain connections for equipment you buy or we help you choose.

Rough-in versus finish plumbing

Remodels split into two plumbing visits, and knowing which is which keeps the schedule moving.

Rough-in

  • Happens with the walls and floor open
  • Drain, vent and supply pipes go in place
  • Water lines are pressure-tested
  • Inspection often falls here

Finish

  • Happens after drywall, tile and cabinets
  • Fixtures, faucets and valves are set
  • Stops and trim are installed
  • Final leak check and flow test

Who must be licensed in Connecticut

State statute defines plumbing and piping work as installing, repairing, replacing, altering or testing water and gas piping and fixtures. It also covers the lateral lines from a building to the mains and connections to backflow devices. The same statute bars anyone from offering licensed work, even in advertising, without the license.

There is an exemption. The statute lets owners do plumbing in a single-family home they own and occupy, but the work is subject to inspection and approval by the municipal building official and must meet the State Building Code. Ask the Simsbury building department about permits and inspections before you start.

Verify any plumber you hire

The Department of Consumer Protection runs a license lookup at https://www.elicense.ct.gov. Choose the lookup, search by name or business, and look for an active or approved status. Licenses expire every October 31, so a status check is quick and current.

Opening walls in Simsbury's historic districts

The Town lists four National Register districts, East Weatogue, Simsbury Center, Tariffville and Terry's Plain. Older homes in districts like these tend to have a mix of pipe materials from different repairs, so surprises behind a wall are more common than in recent construction. That is a tendency and not a claim about any particular house.

If you live in one of these areas, plan a short survey of the existing lines before you commit to a layout. A camera on the drain and a look at the shutoffs and pipe materials tells us where the work is easy and where it will take patience. Tell us if you have plans to preserve plaster or trim, and we will route lines to limit damage.

Water pressure, softeners and fixtures

The EPA says fixtures work best between 45 and 60 psi, and recommends testing with a gauge on a hose bib with everything off. If your pressure is well above that, we can add a pressure-regulating valve during an install. If it is far below, the problem may be a leak or a partly closed valve.

Aquarion's 2025 Simsbury report lists average hardness of 10 grains per gallon, so a softener is a common request. EPA advises annual professional service on softeners and checking salt levels. Not every address uses Aquarion, so test your tap before sizing a unit.
