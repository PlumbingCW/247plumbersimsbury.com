Simsbury Center is the activity center of the town, strung along Hopmeadow Street, which was the College Highway and is now Route 10. Its ZIP code is 06070. Phelps Tavern Museum stands at 800 Hopmeadow Street and the library at 725, so most people in town know the stretch.

What sets the center apart for plumbing is the mix. Houses, shops and offices sit side by side, and a building often holds more than one household or business. That makes the first question who owns which shutoff and which pipe.

A district of houses and storefronts

The Simsbury Center Historic District, on the National Register, runs roughly along Hopmeadow Street from West Street to Massaco Street. The Town says it has held a mix of residential, mercantile and business buildings since the seventeenth century, and that the high quality of its residential buildings is its distinguishing feature.

One real estate site puts the median year built for the wider Simsbury neighborhood at 1972 and says the colonials there date from the 1880s through the 1980s. A mixed era means mixed plumbing, and the date of a house does not tell you what is inside its walls.

Shared buildings and who owns the pipe

A tenant who finds a backed-up floor drain should tell the building owner before anything else, since the owner usually controls the shutoff and the line. Owners of mixed buildings should know where each unit's valve is. Plumbers lose time when nobody can say which valve feeds which space.

For shops and restaurants along the corridor, commercial plumbing calls often start with a floor drain, a restroom or a kitchen line. A drain cleaning visit with a camera shows whether the blockage is in the fixture or deeper in the building line.

Finding a leak without opening the plaster

In a house with older plaster walls, the aim is to find the leak before anyone cuts. Meter tests show whether water is moving, acoustic listening gear narrows the spot, and thermal imaging can show a cold or wet area behind a finish. Then the repair can be a small opening in the right place instead of a large one in the wrong place.

Neighbors

Say whether other tenants share the line. A blockage can affect several units at once.

4

Sewer

Ask the building owner whether the property is on public sewer. The Town's Water Pollution Control Facility can confirm it.
