A sewer inspection in Simsbury, CT is a video look inside the pipe that carries waste from your house to the town sewer or to your septic tank. A small waterproof camera is pushed through a cleanout, and a plumber watches the picture, notes distances and records what the pipe looks like. It is the only way to know the condition of a line you cannot see.

People book one for four main reasons. Buyers want to know what they are inheriting, owners with repeat clogs want a cause, a sewer smell needs an explanation, and a landscape or addition project needs to avoid the line. Most inspections are planned visits, and if a failure turns urgent, the 24/7 line is there.

Before you buy a Simsbury house: why the line deserves a look

A standard home inspection looks at what can be seen, and the sewer lateral is buried. A buyer who skips the camera accepts the risk of roots, a cracked section or a belly that only shows up after the first heavy laundry weekend. Since the pipes inside a building and the lateral to the main are the owner's responsibility in Simsbury, a bad line becomes your cost the day you close.

Ask the seller whether the property is on the public sewer or on septic. The town's Water Pollution Control Facility at 860-658-3258 can confirm whether an address is in the sewer service area, and the Farmington Valley Health District at 860-352-2333 handles septic. A septic property needs the camera for the house line and a separate tank inspection by a septic company.

Repeat clogs, sewer smells and other reasons to book

Clogs that keep returning

If a line has been snaked more than once, something in the pipe is holding debris. The camera shows roots, a low spot or heavy scale, which tells you whether to clean, repair or watch.

A sewer smell

Dry traps and vents cause many smells, but a cracked or open line can too. The camera rules the line in or out so you stop chasing the wrong cause.

Landscape, patio or addition

Digging near an unknown line is how pipes get hit. Locating the line and checking its depth and condition before a project saves a repair after it.

Before selling or refinancing

A recording that shows a sound line is a useful document for a buyer. One that shows a problem lets you decide on repairs before an offer comes in.

What the camera can and cannot see

It can show

  • Root intrusion at joints and cracks.
  • Cracks, offset joints and broken sections.
  • Bellies where water sits in a low spot.
  • Scale, grease and debris along the wall.
  • Distance from the cleanout to each problem.

It cannot show

  • The condition of the soil or voids outside the pipe.
  • A pipe with water so deep the lens is submerged.
  • Anything past a full blockage or collapse.
  • Water supply leaks, which are a separate system.
  • The inside of a septic tank, which needs its own inspection.

Finding the cleanout, and locating the line from above

The camera usually enters through a cleanout, a capped pipe in a basement, crawl space or yard. If there is no cleanout, a plumber can go in through a pulled toilet, which takes more time. We may also suggest adding a cleanout, which makes every later inspection simpler.

To trace the line from above, the camera head carries a small transmitter and a handheld receiver picks up its signal at the surface. That marks the line's path and depth on the ground, which is what a landscaper or excavator needs. The marks are for planning only. Anyone digging with power equipment in Connecticut must still call 811 at least two full business days beforehand so the utilities mark their lines.

Using the video in a sale or a repair decision

Buyers can use a recording to ask the seller to repair the line, adjust the price or walk away. Sellers can use one to show the line is sound or to repair a known problem before listing. Either way, an honest video beats a guess.

For a repair decision, the footage lets you compare quotes. Show the same video to any contractor and ask them to explain what they would do about the specific defect at a stated distance. If the camera finds buildup only, hydro jetting may be all you need.

How to ask for the video

Ask before the visit, so the recording is part of the job.

  • Request the full recording, not only a clip of the defects.
  • Ask that the footage show the distance counter, so each finding has a position.
  • Ask for a short written note of what was seen and where.
  • Ask how long the file will be kept and in what format it will be shared.
  • Keep a copy with your closing papers or home records.

What an inspection will not tell you

A camera inspection is a snapshot of one line on one day. It will not tell you about a leak in the water supply pipes, which is a job for leak detection, and it will not replace a septic tank inspection.
