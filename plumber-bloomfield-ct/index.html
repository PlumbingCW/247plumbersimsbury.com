Bloomfield's housing stock is varied in age. The 2020 to 2024 American Community Survey counts 9,821 housing units in town, and the 1950s are the largest single decade with 2,309 homes. The median home was built in 1970.

That puts a lot of pipe, solder and tank-style water heaters at an age where failures stop being surprises. We handle [[repiping]] and water heater replacement for Bloomfield homeowners, plus burst pipes and active leaks at any hour.

Bloomfield sits southeast of our Simsbury base, about 7.0 miles and about 15 minutes by road. Those are estimates without traffic, so a rush hour trip on Route 189 or Blue Hills Avenue can run longer.

Ranches, Capes and the pipes inside them

These figures are Census data from the 2020 to 2024 American Community Survey, so treat them as a town-wide picture and not a verdict on your street. Bloomfield's own draft plan describes the Wintonbury area as mostly ranch, Cape Cod and Colonial homes from the mid-20th century. If you live in one, your plumbing is probably on its second or third round of fixtures.

A house from the 1950s or 1960s can have a mix of pipe materials, and a plumber can identify yours. Weak pressure at the upstairs shower while the kitchen still runs fine is worth a plumber's look. Homes built before 1988 may have lead solder at copper joints, so a plumber can check.

MDC water and what it does to your pipes

Bloomfield is one of eight towns served by the Metropolitan District, known as the MDC. Its water comes entirely from surface sources, the Barkhamsted and Nepaug reservoirs, and is filtered at the Reservoir No. 6 plant right here in Bloomfield. The 2025 MDC water quality report lists an average pH of 7.41, with a range of 7.13 to 7.99, and calcium averaging 3.09 ppm.

The report says corrosion control is used to bring pH above 7.0, and it lists phosphate at 1.10 ppm as a corrosion inhibitor. In plain terms, the water is treated to be gentle on metal pipes. The report prints calcium but no hardness figure, so test your tap if scale is a concern. An old tank can fail from corrosion and age rather than from a crust of mineral.

The MDC does not publish a hardness number, so test your own tap before sizing a softener. It also states it uses no lead pipes in its distribution system, and adds that lead can enter water from materials in your home's own plumbing. Connecticut's health department says lead solder may be present in homes built before 1988, which takes in a large share of Bloomfield's housing. Ask for lead-free fittings whenever pipe is replaced.

Septic pump-outs in a sewered town

The MDC also provides sewer service, but not every Bloomfield address is connected. For owners who do not have access to the MDC sanitary sewer, the MDC reimburses septic tank pumping up to $275 every three years. Check the MDC billing page for the current rules before you book a pump-out.

If you are on septic, keep a record of pump-out dates, and tell us if a basement drain gurgles or a toilet backs up. Bloomfield publishes no street-by-street list of sewered areas that we could confirm, so call the MDC to learn which side of the line you are on.

Bloomfield services we lead with

Emergency dispatch available around the clock covers all of these, though most are booked for the same day when schedules allow.

Repiping

Replacing worn supply lines with new copper or PEX, done room by room or whole-house. Walls are opened only where needed, and the water is shut off for a planned window, not left off overnight.

Water heater replacement

Swapping an old tank for a new tank or a tankless unit. DOE says most storage tanks last 10 to 15 years, so a heater from the 1990s is living on borrowed time.

Leak detection and burst pipes

Meter tests, acoustic listening and thermal imaging, then leak detection and repair. A burst pipe is an emergency at any hour, and the 24/7 line stays open.

Sewer and drains

Camera inspection and drain cleaning for older cast iron and clay lines where roots and scale have narrowed the pipe.

Repair or repipe, and tank or tankless

Neither answer is right for every house. We lay out the trade-offs and you decide.

Repair and keep the old system

  • Lower cost today for a single pinhole or joint leak
  • Makes sense when the rest of the pipe tests sound
  • Risk of a second leak nearby within a few years
  • An older tank deserves an honest repair-or-replace conversation

Replace with new pipe or a new heater

  • One planned job instead of repeated emergency calls
  • Fresh lead-free fittings throughout
  • Walls and ceilings opened only at access points
  • Tankless needs gas, venting and electrical checks first

Before we arrive in Bloomfield

If water is running where it should not, do these in order and keep the 24/7 line free so we can reach you.

1

Find the MDC shutoff

Close the main valve where the line enters your basement, usually near the meter. In a house this old the valve can stick, so turn it slowly and tell us if it will not close.

2

Kill power near water

If water reaches the panel, outlets or a basement freezer, stay out and leave the breaker alone unless the floor is dry. Call the electric utility if you cannot reach the box safely.

3

Clear the area

Move rugs, boxes and anything stored on a basement floor. Open a faucet at the lowest point to drain what remains in the pipes.

4

Note what you saw

Write down which fixtures lost pressure, whether the water was brown, and when the leak started. Photos of a stained ceiling help us find the line.

Drainage, heavy rain and basements

The town's own hazard plan notes a rise in precipitation and drainage-related flooding, with some problems concentrated in denser areas of older housing. Four state flood control reservoirs sit within town in the Park River watershed. If your basement takes water in a hard rain, a working sump pump is the first thing to check.

Bloomfield also publishes an exempt-work list. It says stopping leaks in pipes and clearing stoppages generally needs no permit, but replacing or rearranging pipes or fixtures does. Inspections must be booked at least 48 hours ahead through the Building Division. We will tell you what applies to your job.

Neighbours and the Penwood ridge

Penwood State Park, at 560 Simsbury Road, is in Bloomfield, and the ridge roads near it are part of our coverage like the rest of town.
