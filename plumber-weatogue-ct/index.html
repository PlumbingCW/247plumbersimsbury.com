Weatogue lies in the southern part of Simsbury, with Simsbury Center to the northeast and West Simsbury to the northwest. Hopmeadow Street, Canal Street and Stratton Brook Road meet at the original settlement in the eastern part of the village, and Route 167 and Town Forest Road also run through it.

The name comes from the Massacoe word for home place, according to the Town of Simsbury. Today it is a residential village of houses on town streets, and this page covers the plumbing calls that suit that kind of neighborhood.

A Simsbury address with its own ZIP

Weatogue has its own post office and ZIP code, 06089, but the town is still Simsbury. Many callers write Weatogue on a form and Simsbury on a bill, and either is fine. Nearby West Simsbury uses 06092 and the center of town uses 06070, so a street address helps more than a ZIP when you describe where you are.

East Weatogue is a separate National Register historic district along East Weatogue Street, with working farms and older farmhouses. If your house is on that street, say so, because the road has its own flooding history described below.

Brooks on every side

Public records place the Farmington River on Weatogue's eastern edge and Minister Brook, Second Brook and Stratton Brook along other borders. The regional hazard plan says Sandy flooding blocked Town Forest Road, and that Lucy Brook overtopped East Weatogue Street in the summer 2021 storms, when small drainage systems were overwhelmed. None of that says a given basement floods.

What it does say is that water moves through this village after hard rain. A sump pump that has sat idle for a dry year should be tested before the forecast. Downspouts that dump beside the foundation are worth redirecting, and that costs you nothing but an afternoon.

Common jobs in a village of family houses

No hot water

A tank that stops heating, trips or rumbles is a job for water heater repair. A cold shower is usually the first sign.

A tank with a leak at the base

Water pooling under a tank usually means the shell has failed, and that points to water heater replacement rather than another repair.

Kitchen or tub drain that crawls

A slow drain that returns a week after clearing needs a look past the trap. Drain cleaning with a cable and camera shows whether the line itself is the problem.

Pump that will not start

A sump pump that hums or never kicks on needs attention before the next storm, not during it.

Repair or replace the water heater

The call depends on the symptom, not only the age of the tank.

Repair tends to fit when

  • The tank is dry and the fault is a thermostat, element or igniter.
  • Water is hot but runs out quickly.
  • The unit is not leaking anywhere.

Replacement tends to fit when

  • Water is leaking from the tank body.
  • Rusty hot water comes from every tap.
  • Repairs keep returning in the same season.

Who owns the pipe under the lawn

Simsbury's guidance is that the pipes inside a building and the laterals running to the main sewer are the property owner's responsibility to maintain. When a lateral needs repair, a plumber must do it, and a camera shows where the damage sits. If sewage is visible in the street, report it to the Town, since that may be a public main.
