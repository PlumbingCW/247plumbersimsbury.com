Tariffville sits in the northeastern corner of Simsbury, inside a sharp bend of the Farmington River that forms its northeastern and northwestern edges. Route 315 crosses the village as Elm Street and Tariffville Road, and Route 189, Hartford Avenue, runs through its eastern end. The post office ZIP code is 06081.

This page is for homeowners in the village who need a plumber for a drain, a leak, a water heater or a wet basement. It sticks to what public sources say about Tariffville and to plumbing advice that holds in any house, so it does not guess which pipes sit under your floor.

A mill village laid out around the river

The Town of Simsbury describes Tariffville as a mill village whose existence depended on water power from the Farmington River gorge and the Tariff Act of 1824. A carpet mill went up along the river in the 1820s, and the company built worker housing, some of which survives. The village keeps the mill housing and street layout of an early nineteenth century mill village, along with later Greek Revival and Gothic Revival buildings.

The Tariffville Historic District is on the National Register of Historic Places. A village that old has seen generations of kitchens and bathrooms added, so the pipes in one house can come from several eras. Nothing public says what any particular Tariffville house contains, and a camera inspection of the main drain shows what is really there before a remodel.

When the river and the roads set the schedule

The National Weather Service gauge on the Farmington River at Tariffville recorded a crest of 9.67 feet on August 29, 2011, after Tropical Storm Irene, and the service lists minor flood stage at 9 feet. The region's 2014 hazard plan says Route 315 closed during Irene, isolating the village. A later plan mentions an undated flash flood on Route 189 that limited access to Tariffville.

Those records describe roads and a river, not any one basement. The practical points are general. Have a sump pump tested before the forecast rather than after it, know where the main shutoff is, and tell us if the roads into the village are closed, because reaching you may take longer.

Find these out before you call

Four quick answers shorten any Tariffville call and change what the repair looks like.

  • Who bills you for water. The Town lists Aquarion, Connecticut Water and the Tariffville Water Commission as providers and does not say which streets each one serves.
  • Whether your address is in the sewer service area. The Town says not every Simsbury property is connected, and its Water Pollution Control Facility can confirm yours.
  • Whether you have a private well or septic system. Those fall under the Farmington Valley Health District, not the Town.
  • Where water shows up in the basement and whether it follows rain. Water after storms points to drainage, while water in dry weather points to a pipe.
