Know the difference

What counts as a plumbing emergency, and what to do first

Treat these as emergencies

Call the 24/7 line right away for any of the situations below. For the first few minutes, your job is to limit the water and keep people safe.

  • A burst or frozen pipe, or any leak you cannot stop with a fixture shutoff. Close the main valve, then open one low faucet to relieve pressure.
  • Sewage coming up through a floor drain, tub or toilet. Stop using water, keep children and pets away, and do not flush.
  • A leaking or flooding water heater. Shut the cold supply to the tank, and switch off its power or gas control if you can reach it dry.
  • Water near outlets, a panel or appliances. Do not step into standing water to reach a switch. Tell us when you call.
  • No hot water in freezing weather, or a boiler that has quit. Leave faucets dripping on exterior walls while you wait.

Plumbing emergency right now?

Call the 24/7 line. Same-day appointments are offered when available, and you can ask what to do first.

Simsbury specifics

Simsbury places, and what they mean for your pipes

A town this spread out has more than one plumbing story. These are the places we hear about most, and the practical point behind each one.

Hopmeadow Street and Simsbury Center

Route 10 runs from the library at 725 Hopmeadow to our own door at 883. Shops and offices along it need after-hours repairs, so tell dispatch if the building has tenants or a shared shutoff.

Weatogue and East Weatogue

Lucy Brook overtopped East Weatogue Street in the summer 2021 storms, and Route 185 closed there during Irene. If your basement takes water in heavy rain, have the sump pump tested before the forecast, not after.

West Simsbury

West Simsbury is its own post office and ZIP, 06092. Give dispatch your street address as well as the ZIP, because 06070 and 06092 both carry Simsbury homes.

Tariffville

A flash flood once limited access to Tariffville on Route 189, and Route 315 closed during Irene, isolating the village. Keep a basic repair plan ready, since reaching you may take longer in a storm.

Terry's Plain

The first Simsbury settlements were here, so older houses may hold older plumbing. If you see green crusting on supply pipes or a recurring drip, a leak check can save a wall.

The Farmington River

Riverside Road has seen riverbank movement in recent years, and the Drake Hill Road Bridge, built in 1892, crosses the water nearby. Homes near the river should treat a wet basement after rain as a drainage question.

Talcott Mountain and Heublein Tower

The state park address on Summit Ridge Drive uses ZIP 06070, and the tower trail is walking only. Homes on steeper ground deal with runoff, so keep gutters clear and downspouts pointed away from the foundation.

Before you dial

Have these ready when you call

We have no reviews to hand you, so we would rather earn trust on the phone. Answers to these five questions help a dispatcher match the right plumber and tools.

1

Your address

Street and number, plus the village if you know it. It tells us the town, ZIP and likely water utility.

2

What the problem is

A leak, a clog, no hot water, a smell or a noise. Say where it is and when it started.

3

Where the main shutoff is

Usually near the meter or where the line enters the basement. If you cannot find it, say so.

4

Water or electrical risk

Tell us if water is on the floor, near outlets, or near the panel. That changes the first instruction we give.

5

Photos, if it is safe

A picture of the leak, the tank label or the drain helps. Never take one while standing in water.
