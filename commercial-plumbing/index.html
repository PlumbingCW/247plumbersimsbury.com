If water is coming up through a floor drain or pouring from a ceiling tile, treat it as an emergency. Our 24/7 emergency line is open, so call while you work through the basics. Close the main shutoff if water is spraying, stop using every fixture on the affected line, keep staff and customers away from wet floors, and stay clear of outlets, panels and equipment sitting in water.

Commercial plumbing in Simsbury, CT means the pipes, drains, fixtures and water heating equipment inside a business or multifamily building, plus the lateral that leaves it. The stakes are different from a house. A single clogged drain can stop a kitchen, and a leak over a suite can spread to a neighbor's inventory by morning.

We work with owners, tenants and property managers. Same-day appointments are available when the schedule allows, and the after-hours line is there for the problems that cannot wait until Monday.

Floor drain backup in a business: what to do first

A floor drain that gurgles, fills or pushes water up is telling you the line downstream is blocked or overloaded. Stop running sinks, dishwashers, mop sinks and restroom fixtures right away, because every gallon you add ends up on the floor. If the water looks dark or smells like sewage, keep people out. Smaller blockages are usually a drain cleaning job.

The Connecticut Department of Public Health advises turning off power where there is standing water or a chance of wires touching it. It also says not to flush toilets during a backup. For a commercial space that means shutting down the circuit for the affected area before anyone wades in, and calling the electric utility if the main must be switched off while water is present.

Is it your pipe or the town's main

Simsbury's Water Pollution Control office says the piping inside a building and the lateral to the main sewer belong to the owner, and lateral repairs need a licensed plumber. Report visible sewage in the street to that office, since it may be a public main. Not every property is on public sewer, so ask them or the Farmington Valley Health District which system you use. A stubborn lateral blockage is a job for sewer line cleaning.

Where commercial calls come from

Most after-hours business calls fall into a handful of failures. Knowing which one you have helps you describe it on the phone.

Restroom outages

A dead restroom is a legal and customer problem as much as a plumbing one. Common causes are a failed flush valve, a stopped branch drain or a closed stop that someone bumped during cleaning.

Ceiling and wall leaks

Stained tiles usually trace to a supply joint, a drain connection or a condensate line above. We trace the wet path before cutting anything, so the opening stays small.

Water heaters and boilers

Tank heaters in service kitchens and salons work hard and fail at the bottom seam. Boilers that lose pressure or leak at a union can leave a whole floor cold. For heat-side problems see boiler repair.

Floor drains and mop sinks

These carry grit, grease and cleaning residue, so they clog quietly and then all at once. A cable or jetting pass clears the line, and a camera shows whether it will return.

Multifamily stacks

In apartment buildings one blocked vertical stack can back up the lowest unit first. We work with the manager to find which units share the line. Our crews also serve Bloomfield and the nearby towns if you manage buildings in more than one.

Hopmeadow Street and the older village buildings

Hopmeadow Street, also Route 10 and once called the College Highway, is Simsbury's main commercial street. Our own address is on it, so most of this corridor is close to home. Tenant spaces here change hands often, which matters because a new tenant rarely knows where the shutoffs are. Walk your space now and tag the main, the water heater stop and the floor drain cleanouts.

Simsbury Center and Tariffville are both National Register historic districts, and older commercial buildings in districts like these tend to have older piping and added-on repairs from different eras. That is a tendency, not a rule for any one address. If you manage such a building, expect that opening a wall may reveal mixed materials, and plan for a camera look at the drain before you commit to a repair.

Grease traps and interceptors for food service

Restaurants, cafes and kitchens that discharge fats, oils and grease need a working trap or interceptor, and the rules depend on where the building drains. The Metropolitan District (MDC), which serves some Connecticut towns, says food service establishments must follow the state DEEP general permit for food service wastewater. It requires a properly sized grease recovery unit or interceptor, keeps FOG equipment cleaned quarterly or when 25 percent of the trap depth is filled, and requires cleaning records.

Those are MDC's rules for its own customers. Ask the sewer authority that serves your Simsbury address what applies to you, and keep cleaning logs either way. We clear the lines around the trap and look for the backup that a full interceptor causes. Pumping out the trap is a separate hauler job in many cases.

Backflow prevention and testing

A backflow device keeps contaminated water from being pulled back into the building supply. Connecticut's Department of Public Health requires certain devices to be tested by a person who completed a DPH-approved course and holds DPH certification. Connecting a backflow device is plumbing work under state statute, so the installer needs a plumbing license.

We do not quote a testing schedule here, because the state page does not set a yearly test for every device. Ask your water utility which of your devices need testing and how often. Boilers, lawn irrigation, ice machines and fire lines are among the connections the MDC lists as needing cross connection control, so those are worth asking about.

Repair choices for a commercial line

The cheapest fix on the night is not always the cheapest over the year.

Quick restore tonight

  • Cable or jet the blocked line to get fixtures working
  • Shut off and cap a leaking branch to protect stock
  • Swap a failed flush valve or stop on the spot
  • Leaves the cause in place if the line is damaged

Permanent repair

  • Camera the line to find the root, break or sag
  • Repair or replace the failed section
  • Schedule it outside business hours to protect trade
  • Needs a plan with the owner or manager
