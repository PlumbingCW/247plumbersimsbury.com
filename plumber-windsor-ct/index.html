Windsor is a river town first. The Connecticut River runs the length of its eastern edge, and the Farmington River crosses the town from west to east before joining it. Water is part of daily life here, which is why basement seepage, slow drains and hidden leaks are worth watching for.

We take sewer line cleaning and leak detection calls in Windsor, along with burst pipes and sewage backups at any hour. Windsor does not border Simsbury. The trip is about 11.9 miles and about 24 minutes, an estimate without traffic, so we plan for the distance and say so up front.

Two rivers, one flood history

The National Weather Service tracks the Connecticut River at Hartford, downstream of Windsor. It is a Hartford gauge, not a Windsor one, but the NWS impact notes name Windsor directly. At 18 to 19 feet, flooding reaches low-lying land upstream of Hartford, including the part of Windsor where the Farmington empties into the Connecticut. Minor flood stage there is 16 feet.

The gauge shows the Tropical Storm Irene crest at 24.78 feet on August 31, 2011, and a provisional crest of 25.96 feet on December 14, 2023. The 1936 record is 37.6 feet. If you live near either river, those numbers explain why a wet spring can leave a basement damp for weeks. Check any floor drain and sump pit before the river rises, not after.

Meadow Brook and the low streets

In the southern end of town, the town's hazard records list two repetitive-loss flood properties next to Meadow Brook, with four insurance claims between them. The brook's culvert was replaced in 2007. The 2024 hazard plan also names Batchelder Road, Pleasant Street and River Street as roads that can become isolated in a flood.

For a homeowner on or near those streets, the plumbing lesson is practical. Keep the sump pump tested, and know where your sewer cleanout is. Windsor's weather page tells residents to call a local plumber to pump out a flooded basement, and our emergency line handles those calls.

Poquonock, Wilson, Rainbow and Hayden Station

The town's own history pages treat these as distinct villages. Emergency dispatch is available around the clock for all of them.

Poquonock

River Street runs near the Farmington River and is one of the roads the hazard plan flags for flood isolation. Street delivery here is 06095, while 06064 is reported as a PO Box ZIP. Check the sump pit before spring.

Wilson

Around Wilson Avenue, Pine Lane and Becker Circle, the L.P. Wilson Community Center is the town's designated emergency shelter. Know your shutoff before a storm sends you there.

Rainbow

Rainbow Road has a fire substation, and Rainbow Brook is one of the town's named streams. If your yard stays soggy after rain, ask us whether the water is coming from the ground or from a pipe.

Hayden Station and Windsor Center

Windsor Center sits around Broad Street and Palisado Avenue, near Town Hall. Across town, 2,078 homes date from 1939 or earlier, so ask for a camera look at older cast iron or clay drains.

MDC water, and the sewer plant in Poquonock

Windsor is one of eight Metropolitan District (MDC) member towns. Its water comes from the Barkhamsted and Nepaug reservoirs. The 2025 MDC report lists an average pH of 7.41 and calcium averaging 3.09 ppm, and says corrosion control keeps pH above neutral. It prints no hardness figure, so test your tap before buying a softener.

The MDC runs the Poquonock water pollution control facility on Poquonock Avenue, though which streets drain there is not confirmed. The MDC says the lateral, the pipe from your house to the main sewer, belongs to you, and it asks you to call its Command Center first when a backup hits.

If sewage is coming up in your basement, stop running water and call us and the MDC. A sewer line cleaning visit often shows quickly whether the clog is in your lateral or the street main.

Wells and septic inside an MDC town

Windsor's Health Department says many residents are still served by drilled, driven or dug wells, and many homes use septic systems. Its septic page links to the MDC pump-out reimbursement form. That reimbursement is up to $275 every three years for owners who cannot connect to the MDC sewer.

A septic home with a garbage disposal may need pumping sooner, because food waste builds up as scum and sludge. Connecticut's public health code also requires a code-compliant septic area to stay available on the lot, so check with the Health Department before adding a pool or shed. East Granby, which touches Windsor, is not an MDC member, so we never assume the same pipes from town to town.

What older Windsor homes mean for leaks

The Census figures are from the 2020 to 2024 American Community Survey. Of the 12,071 units, 2,078 date from 1939 or earlier, and 2,149 were built in the 1970s, the two largest groups. That mix means you will find everything from original cast iron drains to 1970s copper with aging solder in one town.

Hidden leaks in these homes often start small. A water bill that jumps with nobody home is the clearest sign, and a meter that still moves with every fixture off is stronger evidence. We use acoustic listening gear and thermal imaging for leak detection, then open the smallest hole that reaches the pipe. If the pipe itself is failing end to end, [[repiping]] may cost less than repeated repairs.

Sewer line cleaning versus a bigger repair

Which one you need depends on what the camera shows.

Cleaning the line

  • Cable or hydro jetting clears roots, grease and scale
  • Works when the pipe is still sound
  • Roots can grow back, so cleaning may repeat
  • Camera first shows whether the pipe is intact

Repairing the line

  • Spot repair or replacement when a section has collapsed or sagged
  • Needed when cleaning keeps failing within months
  • Ask the Building Department whether your job needs a permit
  • Digging requires an 811 call at least two business days ahead

Before we arrive in Windsor

Whether it is a burst pipe or a sewage backup, these steps protect your home while we travel to you. Keep the 24/7 line free.

1

Stop the water

Shut the main valve at the meter or where the line enters the house. On a well, switch off the pump breaker as well, so the tank stops feeding the leak.

2

Stop using drains

For a backup, do not flush or run sinks, showers or the washer. Every gallon you add goes to the lowest drain in the basement.

3

Stay out of standing water

If water covers outlets, the panel or a furnace, do not wade in. The MDC advises against touching electrical appliances or the fuse box while standing in water.

4

Photograph and note

Record where water first appeared, the time, and which fixtures acted up. If a flood is river-related, tell us before we come, as roads like River Street can close.
