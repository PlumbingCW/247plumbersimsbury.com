Ask a plumber in Granby, CT what the house calls are about and the answer starts underground. The town's own hazard plan says most homes have private wells and septic systems. When the pump, the tank or the sump fails, there is no city main to fall back on.

We handle the plumbing side of all three, starting with drain cleaning and sump pump installation and repair. Our 24/7 emergency line is open for Granby around the clock, and same-day appointments are offered when available.

No water at the tap: well pumps and pressure tanks

On a private well, the pump in the ground pushes water to a pressure tank in your basement, and a pressure switch turns the pump on and off. A pump that clicks every few seconds usually points to a waterlogged tank, while sputtering faucets can mean the pump is pulling air. A pump that never starts may just be a tripped breaker, so check the panel before you phone anyone.

Wells need electricity, and the town hazard plan says plainly that they cannot operate without it. Long outages therefore mean no water and, with a septic pump, trouble getting rid of it. If you lose power, run nothing you do not need and keep a few jugs filled.

Pulling a submersible pump from a drilled well is specialist work, and Connecticut licenses well pump work as its own class. We fix the house side, the pressure tank, switch, valves and piping, and we tell you honestly when the fault is in the well. Test the water after any pump or pipe repair. The state health department says to test the basic indicators yearly, ideally after a heavy spring or summer rain.

Septic tanks that back up or sit too wet

EPA says a septic tank should be inspected every 1 to 3 years and pumped every 3 to 5. Sewage in the lowest drain, gurgling, damp ground over the tank and bright green spongy grass above the drainfield are the usual warnings. A garbage disposal shortens the pumping interval, because food solids build up as scum and sludge.

Septic pumping belongs to private haulers, and design and approval sit with the Farmington Valley Health District, which has regulated wells and septic in Granby from 1976 onward. Our part is the plumbing, clearing the line between house and tank and finding out whether the clog is in the pipe or the tank. A private sewage system also needs a written permit signed by the building official under Chapter 138, so do not start digging first.

Sump pumps and the sewer ban on drain water

Basements in Granby sit among Salmon, Beech, Bissell and Higley Brooks, all of which drain to the Farmington River, and ground that stays saturated after storms. A sump pump lifts that water out of the pit before it reaches the floor. A float switch that sticks or a pump past its years is the usual cause when a basement floods during a storm.

The pump's discharge has to go outside. Chapter 138 of the Granby code bans connecting roof downspouts or foundation drains to the sewer and bars stormwater and groundwater from the sanitary sewer. If your discharge hose runs near a septic drainfield, EPA also says to keep roof drains and sump pumps away from it. For wet walls that a pump alone cannot solve, basement waterproofing deals with the water path.

The October 2011 snowstorm and what it taught Granby

The town plan says the October 2011 snowstorm brought heavy snow, downed trees and power lines, and extensive prolonged outages. It says the storm completely crippled the town. Because most Granby homes run on well pumps, the plumbing effects arrived with the outage, not with the snow.

Hurricane Irene flooded many roads in August 2011, and in 2021 scour took parts of the Salmon Brook Park ballfields and washed out a bridge at Holcomb Farm. Only a small part of town is in the 1 percent flood zone and few homeowners carry flood insurance. Basement water is usually groundwater, so ask your insurer whether seepage and sewer backup are covered. Standard policies often exclude both.

Before We Arrive

For a burst pipe or flooding in Granby, these four steps hold the damage while the 24/7 line gets help moving.

1

Switch off the well pump

On a private well, turn off the pump breaker before closing the main valve, so the pump does not keep pushing water into a broken line.

2

Cut the sump circuit safely

If water is near the sump cord, do not reach in. Switch off that breaker from a dry spot and keep everyone out of the basement.

3

Stop using drains

On septic, every flush and load of laundry adds to the tank. Hold off until we have looked at what is backing up.

4

Keep your phone free

Keep the line open for our callback, and tell us your street and which ZIP you are in, so we find the right end of town.
