A plumber in Canton, CT works by the calendar. The Farmington River and the brooks feeding it set the tone in spring, tropical-storm remnants arrive in late summer, and the winter calls are frozen lines and failed heaters. The town's own hazard plan puts it bluntly. Flooding remains a constant concern in Canton.

That pattern shapes our two lead services, leak detection after water gets where it should not be, and water heater repair when a cold snap or an outage finds the weak spot in an older tank. The 24/7 emergency line is open for Canton and Collinsville around the clock, and same-day appointments are offered when available.

Spring: the Farmington River and the brooks come up

Canton drops from about 1,100 feet in North Canton to about 250 feet in Collinsville, so water runs downhill to the village on the river. FEMA lists severe storms and flooding declarations for Hartford County in October 2005 and April 2007. The hazard plan notes repetitive loss properties in town and two dams on the Farmington River in Collinsville.

Wet ground is when hidden leaks hide best, because the soil swallows the sound and the puddle. If a basement is damp after the snowmelt, check the water meter before blaming the weather. A sump pump that runs constantly can also mean groundwater is simply high, and a dry pit with a damp wall can point to a pipe.

Late summer: Irene, Isaias and Henri

FEMA lists tropical storm declarations covering Hartford County for Irene in August 2011, Isaias in August 2020 and Henri in August 2021. Hartford County is Canton's county in those records. Storms of this kind take out power and leave the ground saturated.

An outage shuts off a sump pump just as it is needed most. It also stops any well pump, and the hazard plan lists helping owners make wells resilient as a high priority action. Electric water heaters go cold with the power. If hot water fails after a storm, check the breaker before you assume the tank is dead.

Winter: freeze, snow load and the long outage

FEMA records a record snow declaration in January 2005 and a severe winter storm in February 2013, and it lists the October 2011 snowstorm for Hartford County too. Hartford's normal January low is about 18.8 degrees. Pipes on exterior walls, in unheated garages and under kitchen sinks freeze first. The National Weather Service advises opening cabinet doors, letting a faucet drip and holding the heat at 55 degrees or above if you travel.

If a pipe does freeze, open the tap, warm the pipe with a hair dryer and never use a flame. Shut the main valve at once if it splits, and see burst pipe repair for what we do next. Standard homeowners policies generally cover burst pipes but not sewer backups. For a stubborn cold line, rerouting the pipe inside the heated space ends the problem for good.

Mill-village houses in Collinsville and Canton Center

South Canton was renamed Collinsville in 1866 and recognized as its own village. The Collinsville Historic District is a compact 19th-century mill town listed on the National Register for industry, engineering and invention, and the Collins Company mill complex sits beside the Farmington River. Canton Center, along Cherry Brook Road (Route 179), has its own historic district, established in 1975.

Houses of that age may sit on stone or brick foundations with water lines threaded through them in different eras. Supply lines added in different decades are where leaks tend to start. Ask for a leak check before a remodel, and where the plumbing is a patchwork, ask about replacing the worst runs rather than patching them twice.

Reservoir water, town sewer and private wells

Connecticut Water runs the Collinsville System, which draws from the Nepaug Reservoir and is treated at the Collinsville Water Treatment Plant in Canton. It has 1,726 residential connections. Its report prints no hardness figure, so ask the company if scale on fixtures worries you. The Canton Water Pollution Control Authority runs the sewers, about 26 miles of them serving roughly 2,000 connections and ending at a plant on the Farmington River.

Only properties inside the sewer service area can connect. A 2004 town plan placed public water and sewer south of the Route 44 corridor, and that document is old. The 2024 hazard plan targets private-well areas for public water, so wells still exist. Tell us which system you are on when you call.

Finding a hidden leak before the bill does

EPA says the average household's leaks waste more than 9,300 gallons a year. Start with the meter yourself.

1

Run the meter test

Read the meter, use no water for two hours, and read it again. Any movement means a leak somewhere.

2

Rule out the toilets

Add food coloring to each tank. If color shows in the bowl after a 10 minute wait, the flapper needs replacing.

3

Let us pinpoint it

We trace the line with listening gear and thermal imaging, then show you where before any opening is made.

Before We Arrive

For an active leak or a frozen line that has split, these four steps limit the damage while the 24/7 line gets help moving.

1

Shut the main valve

Find the shutoff where the line enters the house, close it, and open a low faucet to drain what remains. In an older Collinsville basement, the valve may be behind a stud wall or a boxed section.

2

Kill power in wet areas

If water reaches a panel, outlet or sump cord, switch off the breaker from a dry spot. If you cannot reach it safely, leave it for us.

3

Protect the low rooms

Move belongings off the floor, since water follows the grade downhill. If the river is rising, watch the basement stairs first.

4

Keep the line open

Leave your phone free and tell us your village and street. Collinsville, Canton Center and North Canton are far enough apart to affect planning.
